# Roadmap

This is the one roadmap for the project. It replaces the four earlier 100-item lists as the plan of record; those lists stay in `docs/` as an idea backlog and are linked at the end.

**Goal:** the most comprehensive validated physics simulator that runs in a browser tab. Zero install, shareable by link, embeddable, with every solver checked against known answers.

**First audiences:** engineering education and web developers.

**Scope:** mechanics first (rigid, deformable, fluid, granular, structural), then heat. Electromagnetics is out of scope for now.

## Rules

1. A solver ships with its validation case: a test that compares it with a closed-form answer or reference data and states the error.
2. One unit system: SI. Metres, kilograms, seconds, radians.
3. Solvers plug into one world and exchange forces both ways. A solver that cannot couple is a demo, and is labelled as one.
4. Optimisations are kept only when measured, with the numbers recorded. Rejected ideas are recorded too.
5. New showcase scenes, CPU micro-optimisation rounds and catalogue sweeps are paused until Phases 1 and 2 are done.

## Phase 0: clean base

- Browser acceptance passes for all eight Studio routes and becomes a blocking check.
- The engine suite passes and becomes blocking. It fails today only because `benchmarks/results/engine-100x` and the baseline archives were never committed.
- Every test file runs in a gate. A contract test fails when a new test file is not wired in.
- GitHub Pages is enabled so there is a public link.

## Phase 1: open the engine

Box3D already has the features; the C bridge (`wasm/box3d_web.c`) exposes them and the kit (`packages/box3d-kit`) wraps them.

Done in kit 0.8.0: general bodies, six shape types, three body types, setters, forces, six joint types, events, SI worlds, and a closed-form validation suite (pendulum period within 0.1%, friction stopping distance within 1%, rebound height within 2%, momentum and energy conserved in an elastic collision, sliding and rolling down inclines within 1%).

Done after 0.8.0: the Lab renderer draws spheres and capsules, and convex hulls, static meshes and height fields from each body's own geometry ([`RENDERING_PIPELINE.md`](RENDERING_PIPELINE.md)), and the Mechanisms SI demo shows pendulums on revolute joints, capsule chains on spherical joints with cone limits, a motor-driven hinge and a car on wheel joints.

Remaining: state save/load, and the npm release. Compound shapes and explicit mass data are done.

## Phase 2: one world, coupled

- One scene description and one stepping loop that every solver plugs into, in SI units. The loop exists ([`SIMULATION.md`](SIMULATION.md)): water coupling, structure coupling, heat and solids register with one `Simulation`, which fixes their order and checks their time steps. The scene description ([`SCENE_FORMAT.md`](SCENE_FORMAT.md)) covers rigid bodies, joints, water basins, cables and frames, and loading one returns a ready Simulation. The calibrated cloth, the discrete-element grains, deformable solids (with contact and cohesive fracture) and heat grids are in the format too, and the Lab runs and draws coupled scenes. Still to come: rope and GPU water in the loop, two-way loading of a fracture body by rigid bodies, and the Studio running coupled scenes.
- Two-way coupling, in order of payoff:
  1. rigid ↔ water: buoyancy and drag that push back on the water (done in kit 0.9.0 for one flat-bottom basin; see [`WATER_COUPLING.md`](WATER_COUPLING.md));
  2. rigid ↔ cloth and rope: cloth is done in the kit ([`CLOTH.md`](CLOTH.md): contact with friction, attachments, forces equal and opposite, bodies inside the cloth's implicit solve). Rope remains: cables attach to bodies ([`STRUCTURE_COUPLING.md`](STRUCTURE_COUPLING.md)) but do not collide with anything;
  3. cable and frame ↔ rigid (done in the kit as attachments; see [`STRUCTURE_COUPLING.md`](STRUCTURE_COUPLING.md)). Contact between bodies and members is not modelled.
  4. rigid ↔ deformable solid: done in the kit ([`SOLID_CONTACT.md`](SOLID_CONTACT.md): contact with friction against boxes, spheres, capsules and planes, welds, bodies inside the solid's implicit step). Solid ↔ solid contact and self-contact remain.
- Couplings that share a body: a floating body on a cable or a frame runs, and the structure coupling's interface solve counts the water's force (`linkWaterToStructure`; a moored spar's draught and tension are right to 1·10⁻⁷ m and 4·10⁻⁷ at steps from 1/240 s to 1/30 s, where they were 0.5 mm to 13.6 mm and up to 2.2 % off; [`SIMULATION.md`](SIMULATION.md#water-and-structure-together)). The hydrostatic stiffness is not in the interface matrix, and a floating body on a frame is unchecked.
- Rebuild the bridge study on the validated frame and cable solvers, carrying real bodies in real units. It becomes the flagship. A cable-stayed bridge now runs that way as a Lab scene ([`SI_BRIDGE.md`](SI_BRIDGE.md)); the Studio's suspension-bridge study still runs its authored model, and the document lists what re-basing it takes (a suspension layout with form-finding, SI load cases and readouts, the Studio's renderer and worker).

## Phase 3: missing domains

One at a time, each with its validation case.

- Deformable solids (tetrahedral FEM or XPBD with material parameters), then fracture on top. Replaces the rubber, concrete and lattice one-offs. The tetrahedral solver is done and validated ([`SOLID_MECHANICS.md`](SOLID_MECHANICS.md)). Fracture on it is done and validated for cracks that run along mesh planes ([`SOLID_FRACTURE.md`](SOLID_FRACTURE.md)): cohesive interfaces with node splitting, a crack band, fragments with a one-way hand-off to the rigid world, and crack events in the stepping loop. Remaining: crack paths that do not depend on the mesh (a crack inclined to it is 2.5 % to 30 % too large and dissipates 38 % to 54 % too much), an insertion rule checked beyond the one case it was set on (faces at a crack front are now inserted at 0.4 of their strength, which gives the centre-cracked plate the failure load of a pre-inserted path within 1 % on cells of `l_ch` and `l_ch / 2`), contact between fragments and with rigid bodies, two-way coupling to the rigid world (one-way thermal and dead-load links exist), plasticity in the fracture body (the solid body has J2 plasticity and ductile erosion), and the replacement of the one-offs ([`MATERIAL_FRACTURE.md`](MATERIAL_FRACTURE.md) lists what the concrete and glass studies need).
- Volumetric fluid. A CPU solver now exists ([`FLUID.md`](FLUID.md)): incompressible Navier–Stokes on a staggered grid, obstacles, two-way coupling to rigid bodies and a free-surface option by APIC particles, validated against closed forms, Ghia et al., Hasimoto and Martin & Moyce. Remaining: the GPU port, a turbulence model, sharper obstacles (cut cells), the free surface with bodies and surface tension, fluid in the Studio (it is in the scene format and the Lab: `lab.html?example=sphere-settling`, `cavity-flow`, `flow-past-cylinder`, `dam-break`); sloped beds and wetting/drying for the shallow-water solver.
- Granular on GPU (today: about 1,344 grains in JS in the app, as rigid bodies). A discrete element solver with validated contact laws now exists on the CPU ([`GRANULAR.md`](GRANULAR.md)): Hertz or linear contact, friction with history, rolling resistance, silo walls and two-way coupling to rigid bodies, about 2 million grain-steps per second on one thread. Remaining: the GPU port, non-spherical grains, cohesion, stiffness closer to real materials (the step shrinks with it), grains in the scene format, and moving the app's granular scenes onto it.
- Cloth self-collision and calibrated stiffness. Done as a second cloth model beside the app's ([`CLOTH.md`](CLOTH.md)): a membrane with Young's modulus, thickness and Poisson's ratio, plate bending, an implicit integrator solved to a force tolerance, and point-triangle and edge-edge repulsion, checked against closed forms. Remaining: bending that converges on irregular meshes, an intersection-free guarantee, anisotropic fabric laws, tearing, a solver fast enough for the app's meshes (it holds about 100 nodes in real time), and moving the app's cloth scenes onto it.
- Articulated bodies with URDF and MJCF import. The import is done ([`ROBOT_IMPORT.md`](ROBOT_IMPORT.md)): a robot file becomes bodies and joints of the rigid world, with the inertia gap and every unsupported element reported. Since done: a body's mass, centre of mass and inertia tensor are set from the file, joint stiffness is settable, and a link with several shapes is one compound body. Remaining: a reduced-coordinate solver for stiff chains, closed loops, and opening a robot file in the Lab directly.
- Heat transfer, coupled to fracture. Conduction in solids is done ([`HEAT_TRANSFER.md`](HEAT_TRANSFER.md)), with a one-way thermal stress and thermal-shock estimate. The temperature field now cracks a solid through the thermal link (one-way; the first crack of a quenched plate is 2.5 % later than the estimate, [`SOLID_FRACTURE.md`](SOLID_FRACTURE.md#thermal-shock)). Remaining: cracks that change the heat flow, a resolved thermal-shock crack pattern, phase change, and convection in fluids.
- Later: frame buckling and plasticity (the tetrahedral solid has J2 plasticity; frames do not).

## Phase 4: trust

Runs alongside Phases 1–3.

- A public validation page: every solver against textbook cases, with error numbers. Done for the solvers that have closed-form tests: [`VALIDATION.md`](VALIDATION.md) and the page at `<site>/validation/` are generated from the registry in `validation/` by `node scripts/validation-report.mjs`, which runs each case and prints the reference, the measured value, the error and the tolerance, with a "what is not validated" list per domain. `npm run validation:check` keeps the published numbers from going stale. A new solver adds its cases to the registry with its test (rule 1). Still without cases: the Studio's authored models, the app's cloth, rope and granular scenes, and GPU water.
- A calibrated material library with sources. Done as traceable data, not measurements: [`MATERIALS.md`](MATERIALS.md), `box3d-webgpu-kit/materials`. Every value has its conditions, the figure read and a citation; the six the editor needs that no source gave are marked unverified. Remaining: values that matter and were not found (fracture toughness of steels and polymers, most restitution pairs), temperature-dependent tables, and the Studio's material programs, which keep their own authored values.
- A cross-device determinism matrix.
- Remove the "not engineering predictions" disclaimer one study at a time, as each earns it. The Studio's glass impact lab is the first material lab in SI units ([`STUDIO_MATERIAL_LABS.md`](STUDIO_MATERIAL_LABS.md)): a Kirchhoff plate with brittle fracture (`src/engine/plate.ts`, validated against Navier and Kirchhoff theory), library glass, toughened prestress, PVB and wire bridging. Its "not calibrated" label is replaced by what is and is not modelled. The concrete crush and elastomer labs are still authored: they need compressive crushing (damage-plasticity against f_c and the EC2 curve) and a lab model on the viscoelastic tetrahedral solid (the Prony series is done and validated against the standard linear solid's creep, relaxation and recovery: [`SOLID_MECHANICS.md`](SOLID_MECHANICS.md#viscoelasticity)).

## Phase 5: usable by anyone

Starts after Phase 1.

- Scene editor: bodies, joints, materials, loads (done in the Lab for rigid scenes, [`SCENE_EDITOR.md`](SCENE_EDITOR.md): `lab.html?edit=1`. Remaining: a translate and rotate gizmo, camera panning, applied forces and torques as loads, creating and editing compound shapes and mass data (such scenes open and their bodies can be moved), editing imported geometry, adding or reshaping the coupled sections (coupled scenes open and their bodies and a few section values are edited), and a check on a real GPU and on phones).
- Import glTF, OBJ, STL (done: `box3d-webgpu-kit/geometry` parses the files and makes static mesh bodies, hull bodies, or welded hull sets from an approximate convex decomposition, and the Lab opens such a file as one body; URDF and MJCF robots are read by `box3d-webgpu-kit/robot`; the editor is the item above). Export CSV (done for one body's measured series in the editor), glTF, video.
- Scripting API and console; share links that carry the whole scene (done for the Lab: it opens example links, share links and scene files, and copies a link of the scene on screen; the Studio does not read links yet).
- Docs site with runnable examples and a teaching track.
- WebGL2 fallback for the Lab (done: the Lab draws with WebGL2 when WebGPU is missing or fails to start, with `?renderer=webgl2`, and after a lost WebGPU device without reloading the scene; same streams and passes, no material textures, isotropic metal; same state hashes on both renderers. See [`RENDERING_PIPELINE.md`](RENDERING_PIPELINE.md#webgl2-fallback-lab). Checked only in headless Chromium on SwiftShader). Remaining: a tested support statement for Safari, Firefox and mobile, on real devices.

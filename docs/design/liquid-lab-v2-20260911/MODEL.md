# Liquid lab V2 — model and limits

The studio uses a deterministic 84 × 28 finite-volume depth field across a 6 × 2 m bed. Equal-and-opposite face fluxes exchange mass. Adaptive substeps satisfy a gravity-wave CFL bound within the 1/120 s simulation clock. A uniform bed grade applies downslope gravity; solid cells define posts and winding riverbanks. Material viscosity and substrate resistance damp depth-averaged momentum.

The flume is closed. Other rigs explicitly accumulate pump input, rain input, collected discharge, overflow and infiltration. The displayed balance error is remaining volume minus initial volume and all net exchanges. Rain intensity is converted from mm/hour to metres/second over exposed, non-solid cell area. Permeability coefficients are authored mm/minute allowances, reduced for viscous materials. Closed drainage, zero supply and dry beds are valid inputs.

Pipe delivery is the lesser of requested flow and a fixed 4 m head budget. Diameter, hydraulic length, an authored viscosity-to-kinematic-viscosity mapping, fluid density and attachment losses determine resistance. A laminar/Blasius friction approximation is used. The general pressure-loss treatment follows the Darcy-Weisbach approach described by [US EPA's EPANET documentation](https://usepa.github.io/EPANET2.2/12_analysis_algorithms.html). It is a comparative single-supply model. The on-screen hydraulic length may represent additional upstream pipe outside the displayed bench.

The conservative gravity-wave approach and attention to dry states follow the general finite-volume shallow-water setting described in [Clawpack's Riemann solver reference](https://www.clawpack.org/sphinx-versioning/riemann/Shallow_water_Riemann_solvers.html). No Clawpack or EPANET solver was imported. Independent tests verify volume balance, nonnegative depths, bounded overflow, deterministic state, supply cutoff and expected resistance effects.

The scene's main water surface follows recorded depths. Jet width follows supply volume and gravitational fall speed; rain streak density follows recorded rainfall. Fine normal-map ripples, grass and small stones are presentation details. Riverbanks are fixed boundaries; sediment transport, bank erosion, lateral floodplain inundation, droplet breakup, overturning, contact-angle wetting, curing and thermal changes are outside this model. Surface drag describes liquid mobility and infiltration. It does not establish footwear friction or a certified slip rating.

All 20 profiles are authored comparisons. The names describe appearance and relative behavior; the displayed results are not calibrated laboratory measurements or construction/design certification. The generated mockup contains illustrative material-property numbers that were deliberately not promoted into solver claims.

Snapshots carry source rates and cumulative volume terms, so replay restores rain, jets, surface height, telemetry and supply cutoff together. Saved runs preserve normalized settings, release events, bounded scalar traces and extrema. Older liquid runs acquire default V2 controls on load. Full run export also includes recorded depth and velocity arrays.
